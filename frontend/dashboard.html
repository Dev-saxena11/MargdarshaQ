💡 Why our approach finds better routes

[ 01 ]

There are too many possible routes to simply try them all

Fifteen stops across a city can be ordered in more ways than there are seconds in the age of the universe. No computer can check every option, so every delivery system has to make smart guesses instead.

The formal name for this problem is a capacitated vehicle routing problem with time windows (CVRPTW).
[ 02 ]

The usual guess gets stuck in traffic

Ordinary routing software sends each van to whichever stop is nearest right now. That looks sensible, and it walks straight into the jam — like always taking the first turning that looks clear and ending up in a dead end you can't back out of.

In optimisation terms, the search gets trapped in a local minimum — a choice that looks good nearby but is poor overall.
[ 03 ]

Ours can jump out of a dead end

Our method borrows an idea from quantum physics: instead of only stepping to the next-best option, it can jump straight to a completely different plan on the other side of the map. That lets it escape the dead end the ordinary method is stuck in, and find the route that goes around the traffic. It then tidies the result up — checking its own plan for stops visited in a silly order and straightening them out.

Technique: quantum-behaved particle swarm optimisation (QPSO), with a 2-opt / or-opt local search refinement step.
